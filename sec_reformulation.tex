\section{Dantzig--Wolfe reformulation and branch and price}
\label{sec:math}

Two structural difficulties stand between the 3D-SCPTOP model of
Section \ref{sec:model} and an industrial instance. The first is that
the model carries a complete two-dimensional packing problem for every
truck of every direction and period --- $2|K||T|$ replicas, each
contributing $4\binom{|I|}{2}$ big-$M$ disjunctions
\eqref{sb:eq:nonov-x1}--\eqref{sb:eq:nonov-y2} whose linear relaxation
is weak. The second is that those replicas are interchangeable: nothing
in the data distinguishes one element of $K$ from another, so any two
used trucks of the same direction and period can swap their entire
layouts and loads, and every solution appears in the search a factorial
number of times.

The second difficulty is the one that scales worst, because it does not
depend on the loading geometry at all. It is present in the volumetric
benchmark of Appendix \ref{appendix:A} exactly as in 3D-SCPTOP, and
Section \ref{sec:results} reports what it costs there. Operational
procurement planning lives at horizons of weeks over dozens of trucks,
so that is the regime the method has to serve, and it is the regime in
which a decomposition earns its place.

The two difficulties invite one bad answer, and it is worth naming
because an implementation of the compact model is forced into it.
Rather than let every truck design its own layout, share a catalogue of
$n_{\mathrm{LC}}$ layouts among the trucks of a direction and let each
dispatched truck pick one. That collapses $2|K||T|$ packing replicas to
$2n_{\mathrm{LC}}$, but it is a \emph{restriction} of the model of
Section \ref{sec:model} --- the trucks of a direction must now agree on
$n_{\mathrm{LC}}$ designs --- so its optimum is at or above the true one
and its dual bound bounds the restriction and not the problem. It also
destroys the very structure the next paragraph uses, because a shared
catalogue is a global resource every truck draws on. The compact 3D
runs of Section \ref{sec:results} are of that restricted model at
$n_{\mathrm{LC}}=3$, and are labelled as such.

The reformulation developed below removes both difficulties at once,
and it removes the first without restricting anything. The master
couples to the geometry through nothing but the definition of the two
aggregate flows \eqref{sb:eq:agg-V}--\eqref{sb:eq:agg-Ve} and one count
of trucks per direction and period; every slot, orientation,
non-overlap, stack-height, crush and full-truckload constraint lives
inside a pricing problem. There is no index of a loading configuration
anywhere in what follows, and no variable indexed by an individual
truck.

\subsection{The decomposition}
\label{sec:decomp}

The reformulation is derived structurally rather than by analogy, and
the derivation is given first because every property claimed later ---
that the master is equivalent to 3D-SCPTOP, that $\lambda$ may be taken
integer, that a branching row leaves the pricing problem intact --- is
a consequence of one of its steps.

\paragraph{Blocks.}
Take the block index to be $\mathcal{B}=G\times K\times T$ and inspect
Section \ref{sec:model}. Every row from \eqref{sb:eq:slot-active} to
\eqref{dec:sym-truck} --- slot activation, orientation, stack height,
non-overlap, cover, containment, the full-truckload disjunction in both
its forms, the dispatch row, the slot and empty capacities, the
dispatch gates, the payload and the crush caps, the slot symmetry ---
carries a single direction, a single truck and a single period as a
fixed $\forall$ index or as a literal. Not one of them couples two
values of $g$, of $k$ or of $t$. The exception is
\eqref{dec:sym-truck}, which relates truck $k+1$ to truck $k$; it is
dealt with below.

The remaining rows are the objectives
\eqref{sb:eq:obj-zK}--\eqref{sb:eq:obj-zI}, the inventory balances
\eqref{sb:eq:inv-plant-main}--\eqref{sb:eq:safety-empty} and the
definitions \eqref{sb:eq:agg-V}--\eqref{sb:eq:agg-Ve} of the two
aggregate flows. They see the blocks \emph{only} through those two
aggregates and through the count of dispatches $\sum_{k}Y_{gkt}$ that
$z^{K}$ charges for. The model is therefore block-angular over
$\mathcal{B}$ with no further argument, and a block whose rows never
couple two values of an index is the product of its parts over that
index. This is a property of the formulation and not a discovery about
it: Section \ref{sec:model} is written over trucks precisely so that it
holds.

\paragraph{Delete the quantifier.}
The pricing problem is now obtained by deletion and by nothing else.
Fix a class $(g,t)$, take the rows listed above, and \emph{remove the
$\forall$ over $\mathcal{B}$}: drop the indices $g$, $k$ and $t$ from
every variable, read $g$ as data, and what is left is a mixed integer
programme over one truck. That programme is $(\mathrm{PP}^{g})$ of
Section \ref{sec:pp}; Table \ref{tab:rowmap} is the correspondence, row
by row. Nothing is invented for the subproblem and nothing of the
geometry is left behind in the master. The only rows that do not make
the journey are the two the aggregation disposes of ---
\eqref{dec:sym-truck}, which has no counterpart once the blocks are
counted rather than indexed, and \eqref{sb:eq:lc-active}, whose two
binaries collapse into one (Section \ref{sec:pp}).

\paragraph{Identical blocks.}
Nothing in the data distinguishes one element of $K$ from another: $K$
is anonymous. The blocks of a fixed $(g,t)$ are therefore identical,
and identical blocks are aggregated. In place of one copy of the block
per truck, the reformulation carries one non-negative integer variable
per block \emph{solution}, counting how many of the class's blocks take
it, and one row per class bounding that count by $|K|$. This is where
the second difficulty disappears: the trucks are never distinguished in
the first place, so there is no permutation of them to search.

\paragraph{Classes.}
The classes are the pairs $(g,t)\in G\times T$. Within a class the
block rows are pinned to the class's values of $g$, so there is one
pricing problem per \emph{direction}. The pricing rows read no datum
indexed by $t$: the trailer, the stack types, the crush limit and the
utilisation floor are all time-invariant. The pricing problems of the
periods of one direction therefore differ \emph{only in their duals},
and one column set serves every period of that direction. This is what
makes a column priced at one period usable at another, and it is the
reason the whole horizon costs two pricing problems rather than
$2|T|$.

\paragraph{The dropped row.}
The truck-symmetry row \eqref{dec:sym-truck} of the compact model
orders the trucks of a direction and period so that truck $k+1$ is used
only if truck $k$ is. It is a statement about \emph{which} identical
block carries which load, and after aggregation there is no such
statement to make: counts of identical blocks carry no order, so the
master has no variable in which to express it and the row is dropped.

Dropping a \emph{valid} row is not automatically free --- it could in
principle be the row that made the compact model's optimum match the
reformulation's --- so the drop is discharged by proof rather than by
appeal to the blocks being identical. The converse direction of
Proposition~\ref{prop:equiv} takes a master solution and assigns trucks
to columns \emph{in index order}; the resulting compact solution
satisfies \eqref{dec:sym-truck}. The equivalence therefore holds
between the master and the compact model \emph{with} the row in force,
which is what has to be true for the row's absence to cost nothing.

\paragraph{Discretisation.}
Every block variable that reaches the master is integer. For the
plant-bound block these are the per-slot product loads $V_{in}$, whose
sum over slots is the block's image; for the supplier-bound block they
are the folded counts $V^{\mathrm{e}}_{r}$. A block solution may
therefore be taken to be an \emph{integer} point of its pricing
problem, completed by a minimum-cost choice of the block's continuous
variables --- the placement coordinates $PX_{i}$, $PY_{i}$, which cost
nothing. Discretisation then applies: the multipliers $\lambda$ are
integer, not merely non-negative, and a fractional $\lambda$ in the
relaxation is a genuine integrality violation rather than an artefact
of the reformulation.

\paragraph{The empty column.}
The point with every block variable at zero is feasible for both
pricing problems, has a zero image and costs nothing. It is the slack
of the count rows, which may therefore be written as inequalities and
need no explicit slack variable.

\subsection{Columns}
\label{sec:columns}

A column of a direction is the image of a feasible point of that
direction's pricing problem, together with the point itself as a
witness. Write $\Omega^{p}$ for the set of plant-bound columns and
$\Omega^{s}$ for the supplier-bound set, and index a column by $\omega$
rather than by $p$, which already denotes the plant direction and, as
$p_{h}$, the length of a footprint.

A \emph{plant-bound} column $\omega\in\Omega^{p}$ is a vector
$b_{\omega}=(b_{\omega n})_{n\in N}$ of full RTIs --- the value of the
aggregate $\overline{V}_{n}$ at the point --- for which there exist a
slot count $m\le|I|$, stack types $h_{1},\dots,h_{m}\in H$,
orientations, bottom-left coordinates $(x_{i},y_{i})$, layer counts
$\ell_{ir}$ and per-slot product loads $v_{in}$ such that
\begin{align}
&\text{the $m$ footprints, rotated as chosen, do not overlap and lie
   inside } pK\times qK,
   \label{eq:col-geom}\\
&\textstyle\sum_{r\in R(h_{i})} r_{pr}\ell_{ir}\;\le\;rK
   &&\forall i\le m,
   \label{eq:col-height}\\
&\textstyle\sum_{n\in M(r)} v_{in}\;\le\;n_{h_{i}r}\ell_{ir}
   &&\forall i\le m,\;r\in R,
   \label{eq:col-assign}\\
&\textstyle\sum_{n\in N} w_{n}v_{in}\;\le\;W^{\max}
   &&\forall i\le m,
   \label{eq:col-crush}\\
&\textstyle\sum_{n\in N} w_{n}b_{\omega n}\;\le\;pK^{w},
   \qquad
   \max\Bigl\{\tfrac{\sum_{n}\gamma_{p\rho(n)}b_{\omega n}}{\Gamma_{K}},\;
              \tfrac{\sum_{n}w_{n}b_{\omega n}}{pK^{w}}\Bigr\}\;\ge\;\alpha,
   \label{eq:col-truck}\\
&b_{\omega n}=\textstyle\sum_{i\le m} v_{in}
   &&\forall n\in N.
   \label{eq:col-agg}
\end{align}
Conditions \eqref{eq:col-geom}--\eqref{eq:col-agg} are
\eqref{sb:eq:nonov-x1}--\eqref{sb:eq:cont-y}, \eqref{sb:eq:height},
\eqref{sb:eq:slot-cap}, \eqref{sb:eq:stack-w-plant},
\eqref{sb:eq:truck-w-plant} with \eqref{sb:eq:util-p}--\eqref{sb:eq:utilw-p},
and \eqref{sb:eq:agg-Vk}, read on a single truck. The quantifier of
\eqref{eq:col-assign} is \eqref{sb:eq:slot-cap}'s own: $r$ runs over all
of $R$, with $n_{h_{i}r}\ell_{ir}$ read as $0$ for $r\notin R(h_{i})$,
so a slot carries only the RTI types its stack type accepts. Restricting
it to $r\in R(h_{i})$ would leave an incompatible product unbounded in
the slot and admit columns with no compact realisation, which is exactly
the case \eqref{sb:eq:slot-cap} covers by quantifying over $R$ and
letting its right-hand side be an empty sum. In particular
\eqref{eq:col-assign} is an inequality, as \eqref{sb:eq:slot-cap} is: a
layer may be partly filled. Both pricing oracles impose it as an
equality instead, which is exact on every instance solved here because
their stack catalogues carry one stack type per distinct RTI footprint
and hence $n_{hr}=1$, so a layer is one RTI and cannot be partly
filled. Nothing below uses the equality: the full-truckload floor
\eqref{eq:col-truck} is stated on $b_{\omega}$ and not on the layers.

A \emph{supplier-bound} column is the analogous object
$e_{\omega}=(e_{\omega r})_{r\in R}$ over folded heights $r_{sr}$,
folded weights $w^{\mathrm{f}}_{r}$ and folded volumes $\gamma_{sr}$. No
product assignment is needed there, because the weight of a folded RTI
depends only on its type, so the per-slot crush condition reads
\begin{equation}
\textstyle\sum_{r\in R(h_{i})} w^{\mathrm{f}}_{r}\,n_{h_{i}r}\,\ell_{ir}
  \;\le\;W^{\max}\qquad\forall i\le m,
\label{eq:col-crush-s}
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{sb:eq:stack-w-sup}, and $e_{\omega r}\le\sum_{i\le m}
n_{h_{i}r}\ell_{ir}$ replaces \eqref{eq:col-assign} and
\eqref{eq:col-agg}, which is \eqref{sb:eq:empty-cap}, whose right-hand
side is \eqref{sb:eq:agg-L}; the supplier needs no per-truck aggregate,
because $V^{\mathrm{e}}_{krt}$ is already the truck's own load. \eqref{eq:col-geom} and \eqref{eq:col-height}
carry over on folded heights and \eqref{eq:col-truck} on folded weights
and volumes. \eqref{eq:col-geom}--\eqref{eq:col-agg} is used from here
on as the name of the column conditions of \emph{either} direction,
with \eqref{eq:col-crush-s} in place of \eqref{eq:col-crush} and the
folded capacity in place of \eqref{eq:col-assign} and
\eqref{eq:col-agg}.
Every $\omega\in\Omega^{g}$ costs $c_{\omega}=\lambda^{K}cK$, the value
of $\lambda^{K}cK\cdot Z$ at the point.

$\Omega^{g}$ holds the \emph{non-empty} columns only. The all-zero
point is feasible for the pricing problem and will be needed there, but
its image and its cost are both zero, so a multiplier on it would add
nothing to either side of any master row; it is carried instead as the
slack of the count row \eqref{bp:MP-count}, which is what lets that row
be written as an inequality.

Three features of this definition carry the method.

First, the column carries the \emph{per-slot} assignment $v_{in}$, so
the crush limit \eqref{eq:col-crush} is evaluated on the product mix
actually riding in that slot. A column therefore reproduces the
period-exact cap \eqref{sb:eq:stack-w-plant} of the compact model, and
not the conservative surrogate $\bar w_{r}=\max_{n\in M(r)}w_{n}$ that
a pool of capacity-only configurations is forced to use.

Second, \eqref{eq:col-truck} is the compact model's full-truckload
disjunction \eqref{sb:eq:util-p}--\eqref{sb:eq:utilw-s},
written on the load a truck actually carries; the capacity pair
\eqref{sb:eq:util-cap}--\eqref{sb:eq:utilw-cap}, written on the layers
the truck's stacks provide, is the weaker necessary condition and is
implied by it. A truck is full when it is full on the \emph{binding}
resource --- $\alpha$ of volume \emph{or} $\alpha$ of payload, never the
sum of the two ratios, which is strictly weaker and would accept a
trailer $35\%$ full by volume and $20\%$ by weight. Both regimes occur:
coarse containers fill the trailer by volume, dense ones by weight.

Third, a column is a feasibility certificate. The placement
$(h_{i},x_{i},y_{i},\text{rotation},\ell_{ir},v_{in})$ witnessing
$\omega\in\Omega^{p}$ is retained with the column, so any plan the
master assembles can be handed to a loading crew as a drawing and
re-verified independently of the solver that produced it. Section
\ref{sec:results} does exactly that, truck by truck, on every reported
plan.

\subsection{The master problem}
\label{sec:mp}

Let $\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0}$ be the number of
trucks of class $(g,t)$ dispatched carrying column $\omega$, and let
$\overline{V}_{nt}$ and $\overline{V^{\mathrm{e}}}_{rt}$ be the
aggregate flows the inventory block reads. These aggregates are the
compact model's $Q_{nt}$ and $Q_{rt}$ under a name that says where
they come from: \eqref{bp:MP-def-V}--\eqref{bp:MP-def-Ve} below are the
definitions \eqref{sb:eq:agg-V}--\eqref{sb:eq:agg-Ve} with
each truck's load replaced by the image of the column it carries. The
master problem (MP) is
\begin{align}
  \min\;\; & \sum_{g\in G}\sum_{t\in T}\sum_{\omega\in \Omega^{g}}
                c_{\omega}\,\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}
    \;+\;\lambda^{I}\sum_{g\in G}\sum_{n\in N}\sum_{t\in T} ic_{gn} I_{gnt}
    \label{eq:MPObjective}
\end{align}
subject to
\begin{align}
  \overline{V}_{nt}
    &= \sum_{\omega\in \Omega^{p}} b_{\omega n}\,\lambda^{p}_{\omega t}
    &&\forall n\in N,\;t\in T \label{bp:MP-def-V}\\
  \overline{V^{\mathrm{e}}}_{rt}
    &= \sum_{\omega\in \Omega^{s}} e_{\omega r}\,\lambda^{s}_{\omega t}
    &&\forall r\in R,\;t\in T \label{bp:MP-def-Ve}\\
  \sum_{\omega\in \Omega^{g}} \lambda^{g}_{\omega t} &\le |K|
    &&\forall g\in G,\;t\in T \label{bp:MP-count}\\
  &\text{\eqref{sb:eq:inv-plant-main}--\eqref{sb:eq:safety-empty}}
    &&\text{verbatim} \nonumber\\
  \lambda^{g}_{\omega t}&\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0},\quad
  I_{gnt}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0},\quad
  Ie_{gtr}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0},\quad
  \overline{V}_{nt},\,\overline{V^{\mathrm{e}}}_{rt}\ \text{free}.
    \nonumber
\end{align}
Since $\Omega^{g}$ holds the non-empty columns only, with the all-zero
point carried as the slack of \eqref{bp:MP-count},
$\overline{\lambda}_{gt}=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega^{g}}\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}$
is the number of trucks \emph{dispatched} by class $(g,t)$ and not the
number offered --- which is what Section \ref{sec:branching} branches
on.

The aggregates are written free because no row bounds them below;
\eqref{bp:MP-def-V}--\eqref{bp:MP-def-Ve} equate each to a non-negative
combination of non-negative $\lambda$, so $\overline{V}\ge0$ is implied
and an implementation may declare it either way.
That is the whole master. Objective \eqref{eq:MPObjective} is
\eqref{sb:eq:obj} with the transport term rewritten over columns:
$c_{\omega}=\lambda^{K}cK$ for every $\omega\in\Omega^{g}$, so that
$\sum_{\omega}c_{\omega}\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}$ is $\lambda^{K}z^{K}$
and the second term is $\lambda^{I}z^{I}$. Since every column of
$\Omega^{g}$ costs the same, the first term is $cK$ times the number of
trucks dispatched, at $\lambda^{K}=1$. The superscripted weights
$\lambda^{K},\lambda^{I}$ of \eqref{sb:eq:obj} and the multipliers
$\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}$ are different objects; both names are the ones
the model and the decomposition carry in the literature, and they never
appear in the same expression. Writing the cost as $c_{\omega}$ keeps
the zero-cost, zero-image point of the block out of the sum: it is what
the count row's slack stands for, and giving it a multiplier would add
nothing to either side of any row.
Constraints
\eqref{bp:MP-def-V}--\eqref{bp:MP-def-Ve} \emph{define} the two
aggregates as what the dispatched trucks carry; \eqref{bp:MP-count}
caps the fleet available per direction and period, with the empty
column as its slack; and the inventory balances and coverage-stock
rows of Section \ref{sec:model} are carried over word for word, for
both nodes and both kinds of stock, full and folded (Section
\ref{sec:corrections}).

Two things are worth saying about what is \emph{not} there. There is no
loading-configuration index, and so none of the model's geometric
blocks: the master does not know what a slot is. And there is no
variable indexed by an individual truck, so the symmetry that
\eqref{dec:sym-truck} was introduced to suppress has no
representation to be suppressed in. The entire coupling between the
transport--inventory problem and the loading problem is three families
of rows: two definitions and one count.

\begin{proposition}[equivalence]
\label{prop:equiv}
MP and 3D-SCPTOP have the same optimal value.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Given a 3D-SCPTOP solution, each used truck $(g,k,t)$ induces a load
vector satisfying the column conditions of its direction, obtained by
reading the slot data off its own layout and setting
$\ell_{ir}=L_{g,i,h_{i},k,t,r}$ and, at the plant, $v_{in}=V_{kint}$.
Every condition is a row the truck already satisfies: at most one stack
type occupies a slot by \eqref{sb:eq:slot-active}, and
\eqref{sb:eq:height} with $r_{gr}>0$ empties the slots that hold none,
so \eqref{sb:eq:slot-cap} \emph{is} \eqref{eq:col-assign} and
\eqref{sb:eq:empty-cap} is its folded twin --- which is why
\eqref{eq:col-assign} must be written as an inequality: a partly filled
layer is a legal compact layout. Counting trucks by load vector
gives a feasible MP solution, of equal cost because transport cost
depends only on the number of used trucks and the inventory rows see
only the aggregates, which \eqref{bp:MP-def-V}--\eqref{bp:MP-def-Ve}
reproduce. Conversely, give each truck counted by an MP solution the
layout witnessing the column it carries, assigning trucks to columns
arbitrarily --- the blocks being identical, any assignment serves ---
which reconstructs a 3D-SCPTOP solution of the same cost, feasible
because each column carries a witnessing placement. No catalogue has to
be sized for this: in Section \ref{sec:model} a truck's layout is its
own. Assigning them in index
order makes that solution satisfy the truck-symmetry row
\eqref{dec:sym-truck} as well, so the equivalence holds whether or not
the compact model carries it.
\end{proof}

The proposition holds unconditionally, and that is a consequence of
how Section \ref{sec:model} is written rather than a strength of the
decomposition: there is no catalogue of layouts to be sized, so there
is no hypothesis about its size to carry. It is what distinguishes this
approach from a pool-based method such as the company procedure
PLC-SCPTOP of Section \ref{sec:case}. A predefined pool is a
\emph{restriction} of the problem and can only yield upper bounds of
unknown quality, whereas MP is the same problem written differently, so
any bound derived from it is a bound on the original. The same
qualification applies to any implementation of the compact model that
shares a catalogue of $n_{\mathrm{LC}}$ layouts among the trucks of a
direction: Section \ref{sec:results} compares against one run at
$n_{\mathrm{LC}}=3$, which is a restriction of Section
\ref{sec:model}, so its \emph{plan} is a
legitimate comparator and its dual bound bounds that restriction and
not $z^{\star}$.

\subsection{The restricted master problem and the reduced cost}
\label{sec:rmp}

$|\Omega^{g}|$ is astronomical, so MP is solved over a subset. The
restricted master problem (RMP) is MP over
$\bar\Omega^{g}\subseteq\Omega^{g}$ with integrality relaxed to
$\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}\ge0$; solving it yields duals, the pricing
problems use those duals to look for columns of negative reduced cost
outside $\bar\Omega^{g}$, the found columns are added, and the RMP is
re-solved. If no such column exists the relaxation of MP has been
solved over the whole of $\Omega^{g}$.

Let $\pi_{nt}$ and $\pi_{rt}$ be the duals of the definition rows
\eqref{bp:MP-def-V}--\eqref{bp:MP-def-Ve} and $\mu_{gt}\le0$ the dual
of the count row \eqref{bp:MP-count}. The reduced cost of a
plant-bound column $\omega$ dispatched in period $t$ is
\begin{equation}
\bar c(\omega,t)\;=\;c_{\omega}-\sum_{n\in N}\pi_{nt}\,b_{\omega n}
                              -\mu_{pt},
\label{eq:redcost}
\end{equation}
and analogously with $e_{\omega r}$ and $\pi_{rt}$ in the supplier
direction. The pricing problem of class $(g,t)$ minimises
$c(x)-\sum\pi\cdot\mathrm{image}(x)$ over the feasible points of the
direction's block, so its optimal value $z^{\star}_{gt}$ gives
$\min_{\omega}\bar c(\omega,t)=z^{\star}_{gt}-\mu_{gt}$, and a column
enters when this is negative. In a Phase-I pass $c\equiv0$ and the same
problem minimises the image alone.

The sign convention is worth stating, because it is where a
reimplementation goes wrong silently. With the definition row written
as $\overline{V}_{nt}-\sum_{\omega}b_{\omega n}\lambda^{p}_{\omega t}=0$
and a solver reporting reduced costs as $c-A^{\top}\Pi$ (gurobipy's
\texttt{Pi}), the price that enters the pricing objective is
$\pi=-\Pi$ for a definition row and $\mu=+\Pi$ for a count row. The
prices $\pi$ are irrational in general and must be rounded before
entering an integer-coefficient subproblem; they are rounded
\emph{upwards}, never to nearest, so that the rounded objective
dominates the true one and the value the subproblem returns remains an
over-estimate of the true maximum, as the certificate of Section
\ref{sec:bounds} requires. Rounding to nearest makes it a slight
under-estimate, silently invalidates the certificate, and with reduced
costs at exactly zero returns a value just above the threshold so that
convergence is never declared.

Three further properties of the RMP matter for what follows.

First, it carries \emph{artificial} variables on the definition rows
\eqref{bp:MP-def-V}--\eqref{bp:MP-def-Ve} and on the coverage-stock
rows, penalised at a large cost in the objective, so that it is
feasible for any pool and always yields duals. A solution with
positive artificials is not a plan.

Second, it carries cumulative cover cuts: by period $t$ the plant must
have received cumulative demand plus coverage stock, and dividing that
requirement by the most a single truck can carry bounds from below the
number of trucks dispatched up to $t-lt$. These rows tighten the
relaxation considerably. Their duals, like those of every row the
search later adds, are folded into $\mu_{gt}$ so that
\eqref{eq:redcost} remains the exact reduced cost. This is possible
because a branching or cutting row $\sum_{\omega}f(\omega)\lambda_{\omega}
\lessgtr \beta$ is \emph{robust} --- it leaves the pricing problem
structurally unchanged --- exactly when $f$ is constant within a class
or affine in the column's image. Every row this paper adds to the
master is of one of those two kinds, which is why no branching
decision ever has to be communicated to a pricing problem.

Third, the RMP is \emph{period-scoped}: a column priced against the
duals of period $t$ receives a dispatch variable at $t$ alone, and only
the shared initial pool is offered to every period. This changes the
size of the restricted master, not the master --- by the Classes step of
Section \ref{sec:decomp} the pricing problem is the same for every
period of a direction, and it is still solved per $(g,t)$ over the
whole of $\Omega^{g}$ --- so a sweep that finds nothing at any pair
still proves that the restricted LP equals the master LP.

\subsection{Lower bounds}
\label{sec:bounds}

Care is needed here, because the natural candidate is not a bound at
all. The RMP is a \emph{restriction} of MP: it optimises over
$\bar\Omega^{g}\subseteq\Omega^{g}$, so its LP value satisfies
$z_{\mathrm{RMP}}\ge z_{\mathrm{MP}}$. Until column generation has
converged the RMP value is an \emph{upper} bound on the master LP, and
reporting it as a lower bound is simply wrong. Four rules decide what
may be reported; Figure~\ref{fig:bound} states them as a decision.

\paragraph{A certificate needs a full sweep.}
When the pricing problem has \emph{proven} that
$\zeta_{gt}:=\max_{\omega\in\Omega^{g}}\sum_{c}\pi_{ct}\,a_{\omega c}
\le \lambda^{K}cK-\mu_{gt}$ for every $g$ and every $t$, no column outside
$\bar\Omega^{g}$ has negative reduced cost, so the current duals are
feasible for the dual of the full MP. Weak duality then gives
$z_{\mathrm{RMP}}=z_{\mathrm{MP}}\le z^{\star}_{\text{3D-SCPTOP}}$. The
proof is only as good as the exhaustiveness of the sweep behind it.
Two economies that are legitimate while columns are still coming ---
pricing only the pairs whose aggregates are fractional, and skipping a
pair whose duals round to something already priced --- forfeit the right
to certify, because a pair nobody looked at proves nothing.
Certification therefore runs one sweep over \emph{every} $(g,t)$, with
no memoisation and no restriction to the pairs a branch has touched,
and every call must come back \emph{proved}: an oracle stopped on its
time limit reports itself unproven, and a sweep with one such pair
proves nothing about the whole. Every branching fix must also be
released before the sweep, since a restricted LP sits above the
region's own LP and certifying with fixes in place publishes a bound
that is too high by construction.

\paragraph{The bound is the raw LP value.}
The RMP objective includes the penalty on the artificial variables. The
cost of a plan is the value \emph{net} of that penalty, and that is the
right number to report as a cost; the bound is the \emph{raw} value
with the penalty included, because the penalty is part of the LP whose
dual the certificate rests on. Positive artificials at convergence mean
the region cannot be made feasible by any column of $\Omega^{g}$, so
the region is infeasible in the true problem and is pruned rather than
bounded.

\paragraph{A bound without convergence: the Lagrangian bound.}
A sweep that finds columns still yields a bound, as long as it was
complete. With $\bar z$ the raw RMP LP value and $\bar c^{\star}_{gt}$
the \emph{proved} minimum reduced cost of class $(g,t)$,
\begin{equation}
  \bar z+\sum_{g\in G}\sum_{t\in T}
         |K|\cdot\min\bigl(0,\;\bar c^{\star}_{gt}\bigr)
  \;\le\;z_{\mathrm{MP}},
\label{eq:lagr}
\end{equation}
the multiplier being the class's own count row \eqref{bp:MP-count}. The
expression is formed only when every pair has an entry; a partial sweep
yields nothing, and a pair served by a heuristic rung (Section
\ref{sec:pp}) returns no proved minimum and so contributes none. The
bound is valid at any iteration and is kept as a running maximum. Its
weakness is structural, and it is why the method does not rest on it:
the multiplier is the fleet size $|K|$ at each of the $2|T|$ pairs, so a
reduced cost of $-\varepsilon$ at every pair subtracts
$2|T|\,|K|\,\varepsilon$ from $\bar z$, while the quantity the bound
has to make up --- the distance from the restricted LP to the true one
--- is typically a small fraction of one truck.
Section~\ref{sec:res-bounds} reports what it returns in practice.

\paragraph{The restricted value is never a bound.}
At every node the search first solves the LP over the current pool.
That value sits \emph{above} the region's true LP value and is used
only as a filter: if even the over-estimate lies below the incumbent,
the region cannot be pruned and the expensive sweep is not run. It is
never entered as a bound, and a search stopped by its deadline reports
the last \emph{certified} value it holds, not the last LP it solved.
The reason the qualification matters is that the \emph{best} reduced
cost of a class need not decrease monotonically along a column
generation run: the duals move with every column added, so a pair that
prices out at nearly zero under one dual vector can price out heavily
under the next. ``The oracle found nothing'' is therefore not
convergence unless every pair was proved in the same sweep.

\paragraph{A bound by inclusion.}
The certificate above is expensive when the oracle is the geometric
pricing problem of Section \ref{sec:pp}; the following observation makes
a cheaper one available.

\begin{proposition}[bound by inclusion]
\label{prop:inclusion}
Let $\Omega^{g}_{\mathrm{vol}}\supseteq\Omega^{g}$ be the set of loads
that fit the trailer's volume and the payload and clear the
full-truckload floor on the binding resource, and let
$z_{\mathrm{vol}}$ be the LP value of the master
\eqref{eq:MPObjective}--\eqref{bp:MP-count} over
$\Omega^{g}_{\mathrm{vol}}$, certified as above. Then
$z_{\mathrm{vol}}\le z^{\star}_{\text{3D-SCPTOP}}$, and the same holds
inside any region defined by bounds on the fleet vector.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Two conventions of Section \ref{sec:model} are used and are stated
because the inclusion rests on them: the trailer volume is
$\Gamma_{K}=pK\cdot qK\cdot rK$, and the volume $\gamma_{gr}$ charged to
an RTI of type $r$ in direction $g$ is the volume of its bounding box,
its own footprint area $a_{r}$ times its height $r_{gr}$ in that
direction, with no allowance for void space inside it or clearance
between stacks; by the footprint consistency of Section
\ref{sb:sec:symm} one layer of stack type $h$ then occupies exactly
$n_{hr}\gamma_{gr}=p_{h}q_{h}\,r_{gr}$. Both are what the data of
Section \ref{sec:instances} provide. A valid placement satisfying
\eqref{eq:col-geom}--\eqref{eq:col-agg} then has its RTIs in disjoint
boxes inside the trailer --- the footprints do not overlap and lie
inside $pK\times qK$, and each stack's layers fit under $rK$ by
\eqref{eq:col-height} --- so their bounding-box volumes sum to at most
$\Gamma_{K}$; its weight is at most $pK^{w}$ by
\eqref{eq:col-truck}; and \eqref{eq:col-truck} is the same floor in
both sets. Hence
$\Omega^{g}\subseteq\Omega^{g}_{\mathrm{vol}}$. The rows of the master
--- the two definitions, the count, the inventory and coverage rows, and
any bound on a dispatch total --- do not depend on the load model; only
the column set does. The 3D master is therefore the volumetric master
with columns removed, its LP value is at least $z_{\mathrm{vol}}$, and
its optimum is at least its LP value.
\end{proof}

The practical consequence is the part that matters. A geometric pricing
sweep costs $2|T|$ calls at the pricing limit and, on the instances of
Section \ref{sec:results}, certified no region on any run; the
volumetric relaxation is a two-constraint knapsack oracle over the same
master, and a sweep of it closes in seconds
(Section~\ref{sec:res-bounds}). The method therefore takes its bound from the volumetric
relaxation and asks geometric pricing only for columns. The division of
labour is principled rather than expedient: the plan comes from the
pool, the construction and the tree, the bound comes from a relaxation
whose bound is valid for the problem actually being solved, and the gap
between them is a genuine optimality gap for the 3D problem.

\subsubsection*{The shared block: geometry}

The index $c$ below ranges over the \emph{commodities} of the
direction: the products $n\in N$ when $g=\mathrm{p}$ and the RTI types
$r\in R$ when $g=\mathrm{s}$. Accordingly $\mathrm{image}_{c}$ is
$\overline{V}_{n}$ respectively $V^{\mathrm{e}}_{r}$, the weight
$w_{c}$ is $w_{n}$ respectively $w^{\mathrm{f}}_{r}$, and the volume
$\gamma_{gc}$ is $\gamma_{\mathrm{p}\rho(n)}$ respectively
$\gamma_{\mathrm{s}r}$. The same convention is used in the partition
problem \eqref{eq:part-cap}--\eqref{eq:part-floor} of Section
\ref{sec:relaxfix-flow}.

\begin{align}
  \min\;\; & \lambda^{K}cK\cdot Z \;-\; \sum_{c}\pi_{ct}\,\mathrm{image}_{c}
    && \bar c = z-\mu_{gt} \label{bp:PP-objective}\\
  & \widehat{X}_{i} \le Z && \forall i \in \mathcal{I} \label{bp:PP-slot-active}\\
  & \mathit{LX}_{ih} + \mathit{LY}_{ih} = X_{ih}
    && \forall h \in H,\, i \in \mathcal{I} \label{bp:PP-orient}\\
  & \sum_{r \in R(h)} r_{gr}\,L_{ihr} \le \mathit{rK}\cdot X_{ih}
    && \forall h \in H,\, i \in \mathcal{I} \label{bp:PP-height}\\
  & \mathit{PX}_{i} + \sum_{h}(p_{h}\mathit{LX}_{ih}+q_{h}\mathit{LY}_{ih})
      \le \mathit{PX}_{j} + M^{x}(1-A_{ij})
    && \forall i<j \label{bp:PP-nonov-x1}\\
  & \mathit{PX}_{j} + \sum_{h}(p_{h}\mathit{LX}_{jh}+q_{h}\mathit{LY}_{jh})
      \le \mathit{PX}_{i} + M^{x}(1-B_{ij})
    && \forall i<j \label{bp:PP-nonov-x2}\\
  & \mathit{PY}_{i} + \sum_{h}(q_{h}\mathit{LX}_{ih}+p_{h}\mathit{LY}_{ih})
      \le \mathit{PY}_{j} + M^{y}(1-C_{ij})
    && \forall i<j \label{bp:PP-nonov-y1}\\
  & \mathit{PY}_{j} + \sum_{h}(q_{h}\mathit{LX}_{jh}+p_{h}\mathit{LY}_{jh})
      \le \mathit{PY}_{i} + M^{y}(1-D_{ij})
    && \forall i<j \label{bp:PP-nonov-y2}\\
  & A_{ij}+B_{ij}+C_{ij}+D_{ij} \ge \widehat{X}_{i}+\widehat{X}_{j}-1
    && \forall i<j \label{bp:PP-cover}\\
  & \mathit{PX}_{i} + \sum_{h}(p_{h}\mathit{LX}_{ih}+q_{h}\mathit{LY}_{ih})
      \le \mathit{pK} && \forall i \in \mathcal{I} \label{bp:PP-cont-x}\\
  & \mathit{PY}_{i} + \sum_{h}(q_{h}\mathit{LX}_{ih}+p_{h}\mathit{LY}_{ih})
      \le \mathit{qK} && \forall i \in \mathcal{I} \label{bp:PP-cont-y}\\
  & \widehat{X}_{i+1} \le \widehat{X}_{i}
    && \forall i \le |\mathcal{I}|-1 \label{bp:PP-sym}
\end{align}

\subsubsection*{The shared block: the full-truckload disjunction}

A truck is full on the binding resource. Written on the load the truck
actually carries, with $\Gamma_{K}$ the trailer volume and $\alpha$ the
utilisation floor,
\begin{align}
  & \sum_{c}\gamma_{gc}\,\mathrm{image}_{c}
      \;\ge\;\alpha\,\Gamma_{K}\,(Z-1+\theta) \label{bp:PP-util}\\
  & \sum_{c} w_{c}\,\mathrm{image}_{c}
      \;\ge\;\alpha\,\mathit{pK}^{w}\,(Z-\theta). \label{bp:PP-utilw}
\end{align}
At $Z=1$ exactly one of the two is active and the other is vacuous, so
\eqref{bp:PP-util}--\eqref{bp:PP-utilw} is the disjunction
\eqref{eq:col-truck} and not the sum of the two ratios, which is
strictly weaker and would accept a trailer $35\%$ full by volume and
$20\%$ by weight.

These two rows are where the block's second binary goes, and the
argument is worth stating because deleting a variable from a
formulation is a claim about its feasible set. Section \ref{sec:model}
carries two binaries per truck: $Z_{gkt}$, the truck is configured, and
$Y_{gkt}$, the truck is dispatched, related by \eqref{sb:eq:lc-active}.
With no loading-configuration index they differ at exactly one kind of
point, $Z=1$ with $Y=0$: a truck that carries a layout and no freight.
By \eqref{fix:slot-link}--\eqref{fix:empty-link} its image is $0$, and
by \eqref{sb:eq:obj-zK} its cost is $0$ --- which are precisely the
image and the cost of the empty column $Z=Y=0$. The map from feasible
points to columns therefore sends every such point to a column the
block already has, so identifying the two binaries removes points
without removing a single element of $\Omega^{g}$, and the master,
which sees only images and costs, cannot tell the difference. The
block below keeps $Z$, charges it $\lambda^{K}cK\cdot Z$, and hangs
every load gate on it.

It is worth doing rather than merely harmless. As two variables, $Z=1$
with $Y=0$ admitted a whole family of zero-image, zero-cost points with
the slot, orientation and non-overlap variables still free --- a
symmetry that a big-$M$ packing model solved in the inner loop can ill
afford. And it says something true about the reformulation: in
Dantzig--Wolfe the column \emph{is} a truck, and how many trucks take
it is what $\lambda$ counts in the master, so a second ``is it used''
binary inside the block asks a question the master already answers. One
binary is the right number. (The same observation licenses fixing $Z=1$
when the empty column is wanted out of the way: the empty column is one
analytically known point, of image $0$, cost $0$ and reduced cost
$-\mu_{gt}\ge0$, so it never prices in.)

Four redundant rows are available. Two restate the floor on the layer
\emph{capacity} rather than on the load,
\begin{align}
  & \sum_{h,\,r \in R(h)} \gamma_{gr}\,n_{hr}\,\overline{L}_{hr}
      \;\ge\;\alpha\,\Gamma_{K}\,(Z-1+\theta), \qquad
    \sum_{h,\,r \in R(h)} \omega_{gr}\,n_{hr}\,\overline{L}_{hr}
      \;\ge\;\alpha\,\mathit{pK}^{w}\,(Z-\theta),
    \label{bp:PP-cap}
\end{align}
and two bound the occupied floor area,
\begin{align}
  & \sum_{i}\sum_{h} p_{h}q_{h}X_{ih} \;\le\; \mathit{pK}\cdot\mathit{qK},
    \qquad
    \sum_{i}\sum_{h} p_{h}q_{h}X_{ih}
      \;\ge\;\alpha\,\mathit{pK}\cdot\mathit{qK}\,(Z-1+\theta).
    \label{bp:PP-areacut}
\end{align}
\eqref{bp:PP-cap} is \eqref{sb:eq:util-cap}--\eqref{sb:eq:utilw-cap}
with the block index dropped and the gate on $Z$, and it is implied by
\eqref{bp:PP-util}--\eqref{bp:PP-utilw} because the load never exceeds
the capacity. \eqref{bp:PP-areacut} has no counterpart in Section
\ref{sec:model}: it is not a transcription but a valid inequality, and
it is where the footprint consistency of Section \ref{sb:sec:symm}
earns its place. Its first row says the footprints lie on the floor.
For its second, multiply the height row \eqref{bp:PP-height} by
$p_{h}q_{h}$ and use $n_{hr}\gamma_{gr}=p_{h}q_{h}r_{gr}$:
\[
  \sum_{r\in R(h)} \gamma_{gr}\,n_{hr}\,L_{ihr}
  \;=\;p_{h}q_{h}\sum_{r\in R(h)} r_{gr}\,L_{ihr}
  \;\le\;p_{h}q_{h}\,\mathit{rK}\,X_{ih},
\]
so the volume the stacks can hold is at most $\mathit{rK}$ times the
occupied area; summing over $i$ and $h$ and chaining through
\eqref{bp:PP-cap} and $\Gamma_{K}=\mathit{pK}\cdot\mathit{qK}\cdot
\mathit{rK}$ gives the row. A truck full by volume therefore has at
least a fraction $\alpha$ of its floor occupied. Without the tiling
assumption the step fails and the row can cut off feasible layouts.

All four are implied, and any of them may be added to help a solver
find a \emph{first} feasible layout, which on tight instances is harder
than optimising once one is known. The oracles behind Section
\ref{sec:results} carry \eqref{bp:PP-areacut}.

The direction of that implication is the whole point, and it is worth
being explicit because the reverse is tempting and wrong. Writing the
floor on the stack capacity \emph{alone}, with a per-type weight in
place of the product's own, gives a strictly weaker condition: it admits
a point whose stacks could have been filled to the floor but whose
actual load falls below it, and such a point is not a column. A row over
a concrete load must use that load's own product weights; only a row
written on the layer capacity, which does not yet know which products
will ride in the stack, may use the heaviest the family can carry, and
then only because the product-exact condition is imposed alongside
it. Getting this backwards is
silent in both directions --- the family maximum in a per-load floor
passes trucks that are not full, and the family minimum in a
capacity floor cuts off feasible ones.

\subsubsection*{The direction-specific rows}
\label{sec:pp-dir}

Plant-bound, $g=\mathrm{p}$, with $\mathrm{image}_{n}=\overline{V}_{n}$
and $\bar u^{V}_{n}$, $\bar u^{\mathrm{e}}_{r}$ the analytic slot and
truck bounds of Section \ref{sec:model}:
\begin{align}
  & \sum_{n \in M(r)} V_{in} \;\le\; \sum_{h\,:\,r \in R(h)} n_{hr}\,L_{ihr}
    && \forall i \in \mathcal{I},\, r \in R \label{bp:PP-p-slot-cap}\\
  & V_{in} \;\le\; \bar u^{V}_{n}\,Z
    && \forall i \in \mathcal{I},\, n \in N \label{bp:PP-p-slot-link}\\
  & \sum_{n \in N} w_{n}\,\overline{V}_{n} \;\le\; \mathit{pK}^{w}\,Z
    \label{bp:PP-p-truck-w}\\
  & \sum_{n \in N} w_{n}\,V_{in} \;\le\; W^{\max}
    && \forall i \in \mathcal{I} \label{bp:PP-p-stack-w}
\end{align}
Supplier-bound, $g=\mathrm{s}$, with
$\mathrm{image}_{r}=V^{\mathrm{e}}_{r}$:
\begin{align}
  & V^{\mathrm{e}}_{r} \;\le\; \sum_{h\,:\,r \in R(h)} n_{hr}\,\overline{L}_{hr}
    && \forall r \in R \label{bp:PP-s-empty-cap}\\
  & V^{\mathrm{e}}_{r} \;\le\; \bar u^{\mathrm{e}}_{r}\,Z
    && \forall r \in R \label{bp:PP-s-empty-link}\\
  & \sum_{r \in R} w^{\mathrm{f}}_{r}\,V^{\mathrm{e}}_{r}
      \;\le\; \mathit{pK}^{w}\,Z \label{bp:PP-s-truck-w}\\
  & \sum_{h}\sum_{r \in R(h)} w^{\mathrm{f}}_{r}\,n_{hr}\,L_{ihr}
      \;\le\; W^{\max} && \forall i \in \mathcal{I} \label{bp:PP-s-stack-w}
\end{align}
The asymmetry is the one the compact model already has. A plant-bound
truck needs the per-slot assignment $V_{in}$ because the crush limit
depends on which products ride in the slot
\eqref{bp:PP-p-stack-w}; a supplier-bound truck does not, because the
weight of a folded RTI depends only on its type, so
\eqref{bp:PP-s-stack-w} is a statement about the stack and not about
its contents.

\paragraph{What the method requires of the oracle, and what it does not.}
The pricing problem is the mixed integer programme above, and that is
what the method specifies: the oracle's only obligation is to return an
optimal point of \emph{this} feasible set for the duals it is handed.
The method is stated over the subproblem and not over a solver for it,
and nothing in the master, the bound or the search reads anything else
about how the point was obtained.

The hard core of the programme is the disjunctive non-overlap relation
\eqref{bp:PP-nonov-x1}--\eqref{bp:PP-cover}: four big-$M$ rows per pair
of slots, whose linear relaxation is weak for the same reason it is weak
in the compact model. An equivalent statement avoids those rows
altogether. Non-overlap of the rectangles
$[\mathit{PX}_{i},\mathit{PX}_{i}+\delta^{x}_{i})\times
[\mathit{PY}_{i},\mathit{PY}_{i}+\delta^{y}_{i})$, with side lengths
$\delta^{x}_{i}=\sum_{h}(p_{h}\mathit{LX}_{ih}+q_{h}\mathit{LY}_{ih})$ and
$\delta^{y}_{i}=\sum_{h}(q_{h}\mathit{LX}_{ih}+p_{h}\mathit{LY}_{ih})$ ---
the expressions already appearing in
\eqref{bp:PP-nonov-x1}--\eqref{bp:PP-cont-y} --- can be imposed as a
single two-dimensional disjointness relation over those rectangles,
with no separating binaries $A,B,C,D$ at all. The feasible sets of the
two statements coincide, so an implementation is free to use either.
The runs reported in Section \ref{sec:results} were produced by the
second, through a constraint programming solver that propagates that
relation natively; Section \ref{sec:protocol} records which solver
solved what. That is an implementation choice behind those numbers and
not a part of the method, and Section \ref{sec:results} reports no
comparison between the two technologies: the one on record was made
under an oracle that did not implement the composition bounds the
method depends on, which makes it a comparison between two different
subproblems rather than between two solvers. What matters is only that
the oracle solve \emph{this} problem; an earlier variant that imposed
the full-truckload requirement as the sum of the two ratios in
\eqref{eq:col-truck} rather than as their disjunction solved a strictly
weaker one and returned a column that failed verification.

\paragraph{The volumetric oracle.}
The same master --- the same two definition rows, the same count row,
the same inventory balances and coverage stock --- is also solved over
the loads of the volumetric benchmark of Appendix \ref{appendix:A},
where a load is a column iff its volume fits the trailer's, its weight
the payload, and it clears the full-truckload floor on the binding
resource. Pricing is then a two-constraint integer knapsack that closes
in milliseconds. Running both oracles through one master and one search
is what makes the comparison of Section \ref{sec:results} an experiment
about the loading representation rather than about two solution
technologies, and it is the setting in which per-node certification is
affordable.

\paragraph{What a certificate costs.}
Figure~\ref{fig:pricing} shows the oracle and what it can and cannot
return. A certified node is a full sweep, $2|T|$ calls each closed to
proven optimality. On the volumetric oracle that is affordable at every
node. On the geometric problem it is not --- a single call on the
instances of Section \ref{sec:results} takes tens of seconds to
minutes, and a sweep is then the whole budget of a node --- so with the
geometric oracle the certificate is taken from the volumetric
relaxation (Proposition~\ref{prop:inclusion}) and geometric pricing is
asked only for columns.

\paragraph{A pricing ladder.}
Any column of negative reduced cost improves the restricted master; the
\emph{most} negative one is needed only for the certificate. Column
generation over the geometric problem therefore runs as a ladder. The
first rung is a heuristic run \emph{under the current duals} at every
iteration --- a randomised greedy construction over enumerated stack
fills, placed by a shelf packer, together with a re-fill of the slot
geometry of columns already in the pool --- whose output is pricing
output, never seeding. A heuristic load \emph{qualifies} only if its
reduced cost lies below a margin of half a percent of the pair's
threshold; the best two qualifying loads per pair are kept, and a pair
at which nothing qualifies goes to the exact oracle. The oracle itself
is run in a stop-early mode that returns its first incumbent beating
the threshold by the same margin.

\paragraph{What the margin does, and what it does not.}
The margin is worth stating precisely, because the obvious reading of
it is wrong and we have measured the difference. It tests whether a
heuristic column is good enough \emph{in its own reduced cost} to be
worth adding: it discards the near-zero columns a stalled greedy
produces, which would otherwise enter the pool and pre-empt the oracle
for nothing. It does \emph{not} compare the heuristic column with the
column the oracle would have returned at the same duals, and it cannot,
since that column is exactly what has not been computed. A strongly
negative heuristic column therefore passes the margin and suppresses
the oracle at that pair completely. Three sweeps on
$I\_16\_15\_2\_90\_k25$ at five seconds per call record the extreme
case: the rung qualified at every pair that had an improving column to
give, not one priced column entered the pool, and the exact oracle ---
which in that experiment was run at every pair regardless --- therefore
contributed nothing.

That matters only because the two generators are
\emph{complementary rather than substitutable}, which is the same
$\operatorname{conv}(\Omega^{g})$ argument as
Figure~\ref{fig:hull}. In the arm that calls both at every pair, and so
compares them under one dual trajectory, the oracle's columns carry
$4.72$ distinct commodities on average (median $5$) with $0.522$ of the
load in the heaviest, and the rung's carry $10.17$ (median $14$ of
$16$) at $0.374$: pricing returns extreme, concentrated loads because
its objective is linear, and the rung returns balanced ones because it
is not optimising that objective at all. So running both is not
redundant work. At an equal number of sweeps from the same pool,
running both gives a tighter restricted-master LP than either generator
alone at either per-call budget --- $76\,304.92$ against the
pre-emptive ladder's $76\,839.25$ over three sweeps at five seconds,
and $75\,361.05$ against $75\,983.75$ over six at twenty, the
exact-only arm being behind both in each case. Lower is tighter: the
restricted-master value is an upper bound on the master LP until
pricing converges (Section~\ref{sec:bounds}).

What stops that from being a blanket rule is the price. Running both
pays an oracle call at every rung-served pair and carries the larger
pool, and the master LPs then pay for both: $265$ columns against the
pre-emptive ladder's $185$ at the same budget. Per \emph{second} the
pre-emptive arrangement is the better one at the short end, reaching
$75\,983.75$ in $405.6$ s where the additive arrangement stood at
$76\,498.48$ at a comparable $395.9$ s and needed $799.1$ s for its own
final value. The rule the implementation therefore applies is a
condition on the oracle's marginal cost rather than a preference
between the generators: \textbf{run both while the sweep's mean
per-pair oracle time is at or under five seconds, and let the rung
pre-empt above it}, the mean being a quantity the sweep already
records. Under the per-call budget of the campaign, $20$ s, that
condition selects pre-emption --- which is the structure the code
already had --- so \emph{nothing in the results of
Section~\ref{sec:results} was produced by a different algorithm}. Its
provenance is two per-call budgets on one instance, and the two
configurations also differ in cores, so it is a calibration and not a
law; what the measurements fix unambiguously is its direction.

The certifying sweep never uses either rung, and neither does the
Lagrangian bound \eqref{eq:lagr}: a certificate is one uncapped exact
sweep, exactly as in Section \ref{sec:bounds}, so the ladder changes
which columns arrive and how fast, and nothing about what may be
claimed.

\subsection{Initial columns}
\label{sec:initcols}

The search starts from the plainest possible pool: one maximally full
truck per commodity and direction, obtained in the geometric case by
running the pricing problem for that commodity alone --- so that where
it closes, the pool truck is the placeable maximum of the commodity and
the same number serves as its placeability cap in Section
\ref{sec:geo3d} --- and in the volumetric case by filling to capacity.
The geometric probe carries a time limit like every other, and where it
expires the pool still gets a column but the count behind it is not
proved maximal and the analytic cap stays in force, exactly as Section
\ref{sec:geo3d} states. It is not a formality: on
$I\_16\_15\_2\_90\_k25$ seventeen of the eighteen (direction,
commodity) probes closed and one reached its limit. No demand mixes, no
diversification and no reduced variants are seeded, so whatever the
search then reaches, it reached by pricing, branching and construction.

This is a requirement of the method rather than a convenience: a pool
assembled by hand contaminates every claim about what the search finds,
and an earlier seeded pool was removed on that ground. The
single-commodity pool guarantees that no direction is ever left without
columns, and because these columns are shared they are offered to every
period.

What such a pool, and any pool grown from it by pricing alone,
\emph{cannot} contain is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:hull} on a real pool.
For any dual vector the pricing objective \eqref{bp:PP-objective} is
linear in the image, so it attains its optimum at an extreme point of
$\operatorname{conv}(\Omega^{g})$ and pricing returns extreme loads
only. A balanced load lying inside the hull is dominated componentwise
by a fractional blend of single-commodity trucks; the relaxation buys
that blend for the price of one truck, while an integer plan must pay
for each truck it mixes. No choice of duals returns the interior load,
however the search moves them. Interior loads are precisely what an
integer plan needs when a period's demand does not decompose into full
trucks, and the method therefore has a second generator for them.

\subsection{Relax and fix}
\label{sec:relaxfix}

The second generator, and the procedure that produced every plan
reported in Section \ref{sec:results}, is a relax-and-fix heuristic on
the restricted master. Two such procedures appear in this work and they
are not variants of one another; they relax and fix different things,
they are run in different places, and each is stated in full below so
that neither is mistaken for the other.

\begin{itemize}
\item The \textbf{flow-first} relax and fix of Section
  \ref{sec:relaxfix-flow} fixes the \emph{aggregates} --- the delivery
  vectors and the dispatch totals --- while leaving the column
  variables continuous, and then realises the fixed aggregates as
  trucks. It runs at every node of the fleet tree of Section
  \ref{sec:branching} and is the primal heuristic of MATH-3D-SCPTOP.
\item The \textbf{horizon-walk} relax and fix of Section
  \ref{sec:relaxfix-walk} fixes the \emph{column variables of one
  period at a time}, with a sliding window of integer periods and a
  continuous tail. It was used in the earlier period-scope experiments
  on the volumetric master and is reported as such; no 3D result in
  this paper comes from it.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Flow-first relax and fix}
\label{sec:relaxfix-flow}

The procedure decides first how much moves and how many trucks carry
it, and builds the trucks afterwards. It is run inside a region
$\mathcal{R}$ of the fleet tree --- a set of bounds on the dispatch
totals $n_{gt}:=\sum_{\omega}\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}$ --- and must stay
inside it, or the children the node induces would partition around a
point the node excludes. It runs on a \emph{fresh copy} of the
restricted master, because step 2 changes variable types and doing that
to the node's own LP would leave it a mixed integer programme with no
duals for whatever runs next.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Restricted master.} The LP over the pool, inside
  $\mathcal{R}$, gives the duals and the fractional point the node
  stands on.

\item \emph{Aggregate integer master.} Introduce the dispatch totals
  $n_{gt}$ as explicit integer variables, make the aggregates
  $\overline{V}_{nt}$ and $\overline{V^{\mathrm{e}}}_{rt}$ integer,
  and leave the column variables $\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}$
  \emph{continuous}; impose $\mathcal{R}$ on the $n_{gt}$ as well as
  on the columns. Under 3D loading add the stack-count rows of
  Section~\ref{sec:geo3d}. Solve to a relative gap $\varepsilon_{2}$,
  with the solver set to find feasible solutions early.

  This is where the objective is actually decided, and it is decided
  \emph{without a single call to a pricing problem}: the row set is
  the master's own, and what it chooses is the flows and the fleet. Three
  outcomes are distinguished. No solution within the limit abandons the
  node. Artificial slack above a tolerance means the region is
  infeasible over this pool, so its delivery vectors are meaningless and
  the node is abandoned --- partitioning them would waste the node and
  could return a ``plan'' that does not meet demand. Slack below the
  tolerance means the integer solve ran out of time short of a clean
  solution; the partitions built from it are still legal loads, and step
  4 decides. Write $Q^{\star}$ for the integer delivery vectors and
  $n^{\star}$ for the integer fleet it returns.

\item \emph{Partition, as a cover.} For each direction and period,
  realise $Q^{\star}_{gt}$ as $n^{\star}_{gt}$ legal trucks by a small
  integer programme over per-truck commodity counts $a_{kc}$,
  $k\le n^{\star}_{gt}$:
  \begin{align}
    \min\;\;&\sum_{c}\gamma_{gc}\Bigl(\sum_{k}a_{kc}-Q^{\star}_{gtc}\Bigr)
      \label{eq:part-obj}\\
    \text{s.t.}\;\;&\sum_{k}a_{kc}\;\ge\;Q^{\star}_{gtc} &&\forall c
      \label{eq:part-cover}\\
    &\textstyle\sum_{c}\gamma_{gc}a_{kc}\le\Gamma_{K},\qquad
     \sum_{c}w_{c}a_{kc}\le pK^{w} &&\forall k
      \label{eq:part-cap}\\
    &\textstyle\sum_{c}\gamma_{gc}a_{kc}\ge\alpha\Gamma_{K}z_{k},\qquad
     \sum_{c}w_{c}a_{kc}\ge\alpha pK^{w}(1-z_{k}) &&\forall k
      \label{eq:part-floor}\\
    &\textstyle\sum_{c}w_{c}a_{kc}\;\ge\;\sum_{c}w_{c}a_{k+1,c}
      &&\forall k<n^{\star}_{gt}
      \label{eq:part-sym}\\
    &a_{kc}\in\{0,\dots,\bar u_{gc}\},\qquad z_{k}\in\{0,1\}.\nonumber
  \end{align}
  Constraint \eqref{eq:part-cover} is a \emph{cover}, not an exact
  split: the parts must sum to \emph{at least} the delivery vector in
  every commodity, and \eqref{eq:part-obj} minimises the excess.
  \eqref{eq:part-cap}--\eqref{eq:part-floor} make each part a legal
  truck, with $z_{k}$ selecting the resource on which part $k$ meets
  the floor; \eqref{eq:part-sym} orders the parts by weight to break
  the symmetry among them, and $\bar u_{gc}$ is the placeability cap of
  Section \ref{sec:geo3d}. If $n^{\star}_{gt}$ parts cannot be formed,
  $n^{\star}_{gt}+1$ and then $n^{\star}_{gt}+2$ are tried, provided
  $\mathcal{R}$ allows it. A timeout is not a proof: only a model that
  comes back infeasible says the cover is impossible.

\item \emph{Integer master over pool plus parts.} The parts are added
  to the pool as columns and the integer master is solved over the
  enlarged pool, warm-started with the partition itself. Its solution
  is the node's plan and its value the candidate incumbent.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why the partition must be a cover.}
This is the step at which the procedure could quietly become wrong, and
the reason is worth stating exactly. In the compact model and in the
integer master the packing is a decision variable, so
$\overline{V}_{nt}=\sum_{\omega}b_{\omega n}\lambda^{p}_{\omega t}$
with integer $\lambda$ is a sum of placeable loads by construction and
an unrealisable delivery vector can never be requested. Step 2 breaks
that link: it fixes the aggregates over \emph{continuous} $\lambda$, so
$Q^{\star}$ is a fractional blend of legal loads --- and a fractional
blend of legal loads need not be realisable as legal loads at all, for
\emph{any} number of trucks.

The mechanism is that floor area is consumed in whole footprints while
the utilisation floor is measured in volume. A partial stack occupies
the same footprint as a full one, so splitting a delivery vector into
more trucks consumes more floor without adding volume, while splitting
it into fewer violates the trailer. On
$I\_16\_15\_2\_90\_k25$ the vector $\{R_{1}\!:\!200,\,R_{2}\!:\!30\}$
holds $1.83$ full-truckload floors of volume --- so one truck cannot
carry it and two cannot both be full --- and needs $35.5\,\mathrm{m}^{2}$
of stacks on a $32.64\,\mathrm{m}^{2}$ floor, so it is realisable by no
number of full trucks whatever. Under a cover the same vector is two
trucks of $\{R_{1}\!:\!100,\,R_{2}\!:\!15\}$ plus a few units.
An exact split would therefore fail on vectors the model has every right
to choose, and failing is not a neutral outcome: it costs the node its
plan.

The cover is sound for the same reason the step exists. Every part is
still a legal, full truck and hence a legal column, and the parts enter
step 4 \emph{as columns with a warm start}, not as fixed dispatches, so
the integer master is free to dispatch fewer of them than were built. A
plan that over-delivers is feasible in the master: the inventory rows
are balances with no upper bound on the plant's stock, so the surplus is
priced as holding cost, and the supplier's own stock balance stops a
column that would ship more than the supplier has from being dispatched
at all. The excess in \eqref{eq:part-obj} is measured in volume rather
than in units, because volume is the resource the floor and the trailer
are measured in, so a surplus folded empty and a surplus full pallet are
weighed by what they displace rather than counted alike.

\paragraph{What step 2 must know, and what it need not.}
Step 2 sees the master's rows and the pool, not the geometry, so it can
choose aggregates that no partition can realise. The aggregate
stack-count rows of Section \ref{sec:geo3d} are what keep that in
check, and the failure they fix is concrete: on $I\_8\_5\_2\_90$ over
the pure pool, step 2 under a \emph{linear} stack-area row chose the
right flow --- the twelve-truck optimum --- and then asked the partition
for $\{R_{1}\!:\!85,\,R_{2}\!:\!117\}$ in one supplier truck, which is
$13+17=30$ stacks of seven layers, $32.88\,\mathrm{m}^{2}$ on a
$32.64\,\mathrm{m}^{2}$ floor. The linear row admits it
($11.66+20.06=31.7\,\mathrm{m}^{2}$); the partition refused it; the pair
went to two trucks and the plan to thirteen at $19\,682.36$ instead of
twelve at $18\,796.96$. With integer stack counts per footprint step 2
sees the difference. The rows remain a relaxation of the per-truck split
--- an aggregate that fits $n$ floors need not divide into $n$ --- which
is why the $n+1$ fallback of step 3 stays in the procedure by design.

\subsubsection{Horizon-walk relax and fix}
\label{sec:relaxfix-walk}

The second procedure attacks a different obstruction: the integer
master over the whole horizon. At twenty periods that monolith is what
fails. In the eight recorded runs reported below, over final pools of
between $551$ and $3\,518$ columns --- the range over all eight runs ---
the closing integer master over the whole horizon produced no incumbent
on \emph{any} run, while the walk produced one on six of the eight.

So commit period by period. Let $w$ be a window width. At step $i$ of
the walk the dispatch variables $\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}$ are
\emph{integer} for $t$ in the window $\{t_{i},\dots,t_{i+w-1}\}$ and
\emph{continuous} for every other period, which leaves the tail free to
absorb whatever the committed prefix leaves. The window's own mixed
integer programme has $|\bar\Omega|\times w$ integer variables instead
of $|\bar\Omega|\times|T|$. Solve it, fix the variables of its
\emph{first} period at the counts it chose, slide the window forward by
one, and repeat. The walk runs on a copy of the master, for the same
reason step 2 above does.

Inventory is what couples the periods, and it is forgiving in one
direction: shipping earlier only carries stock, so a committed prefix
rarely strands the tail. That is why the walk is cheap where the
monolith is intractable. When a window does come back with positive
artificials, the committed prefix cannot be completed out of the
columns that window may use --- under period scoping, the loads priced
at the window's own periods --- and two recoveries are available. A
\emph{repair} asks the pricing problem for a load covering the
shortfall the artificials name and retries the same window; a
\emph{backtrack} releases the last commitment, widens the window by one
and lets the two periods be decided together rather than one after the
other. If neither applies the walk abandons, and records which period
and which shortfall, since a walk that returns nothing silently is
indistinguishable from one that was never run.

This is the procedure the period-scope experiments used, on the
volumetric master, and Section~\ref{sec:res-vol} reports what it
returned there. It is described here, at the length its reimplementation
needs, because it is a distinct procedure with recorded results of its
own, and because the aggregate-first alternative above is a response to
the same obstruction --- not because the paper compares the two. They
have never been run against each other under a common budget, and no
such comparison is offered.

\subsection{The fleet tree}
\label{sec:branching}

What may be branched on is decided before anything else, and it is
decided by the criterion of Section \ref{sec:rmp}: a row
$\sum_{\omega}f(\omega)\,\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}\lessgtr\beta$ added to
the master is \emph{robust} --- it changes the duals and nothing else,
leaving the pricing problem structurally untouched --- exactly when
$f$ is constant within a class or affine in the column's image. The
branching hierarchy used here is read off that criterion rather than
chosen and then justified, and it has three rungs.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{$f\equiv1$: counts of dispatches.} Any bound on a sum of
  $\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}$ with unit coefficients is robust, because its
  dual folds into $\mu_{gt}$ exactly as the count row
  \eqref{bp:MP-count} does and the pricing objective
  \eqref{bp:PP-objective} is unchanged. The aggregates available at
  this rung are the per-class dispatch total
  $\overline{\lambda}_{gt}=\sum_{\omega}\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}$, any
  subset sum of them, and in particular the total fleet
  $X=\sum_{g,t}\overline{\lambda}_{gt}$. All are integer in every plan,
  so a fractional one is a genuine violation to branch on.
\item \textbf{$f$ affine in the image: deliveries.} The aggregates
  $\overline{V}_{nt}=\sum_{\omega}b_{\omega n}\lambda^{p}_{\omega t}$
  and $\overline{V^{\mathrm{e}}}_{rt}$ have $f(\omega)=b_{\omega n}$,
  which is affine (indeed linear) in the image, so a bound on them is
  robust as well: its dual is absorbed into $\pi_{nt}$, which is a price
  on the image and therefore already an input of the pricing problem.
  These are continuous variables of the relaxation, but they count
  physical RTIs and are integer in any plan, so they too may carry a
  dichotomy.
\item \textbf{Everything else: column variables.} Bounding a single
  $\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}$ has $f=\mathbf{1}[\omega=\omega_{0}]$, which
  is neither constant within the class nor affine in the image. Such a
  row is \emph{not} robust: honouring it requires excluding $\omega_{0}$
  from the pricing problem's feasible set, which the subproblem must be
  told about and which destroys the property that one column set serves
  every period of a direction.
\end{enumerate}

The third rung is useless here as well as non-robust, which settles the
matter. Bounding
one $\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}$ removes one load from the relaxation, but a
Dantzig--Wolfe master of this kind holds many columns that differ only
in detail, so the relaxation answers by shifting the same fractional
weight onto a near-identical column: the bound
$\lambda^{g}_{\omega t}\le\lfloor\lambda^{\star}\rfloor$ excludes one
point of $\Omega^{g}$ from a set whose remaining points include many
differing from it in a single RTI, so the child's LP value moves by
almost nothing and the fractionality reappears one column over. The
depth needed to exhaust that family grows with the pool, which is what
makes the rule unusable here rather than merely slow. Run on one
benchmark instance with the pool frozen, it branched $3\,000$ times
without ever producing an integral relaxation, and left most of its
regions open.

That leaves the first two rungs, and between them the first is the one
that binds. Transport cost is $cK\cdot X$ and nothing else in the
objective counts trucks, so a bound on a dispatch count is a bound on
cost directly, whereas a bound on a delivery is answered by the
relaxation moving freight between periods at the price of one period's
holding cost --- and a bound on a \emph{single} period's dispatch total
is answered the same way, by moving a truckload into a neighbouring
period. The aggregate that has no such escape is the total over the
whole horizon.

We therefore branch on the \emph{fleet}. With $n_{gt}=\overline{\lambda}_{gt}$
the dispatch total of direction $g$ in period $t$, let
$\mathbf{n}=(n_{gt})_{g,t}$ be the fleet vector and
$X:=\sum_{g,t}n_{gt}$ the total fleet. Every bound placed on either is
a rung-one row and the pricing problems are untouched. Given an
incumbent plan with fleet vector $\mathbf{n}^{\star}$ and total
$X^{\star}$, the tree has two levels that ask different questions ---
level 1 on the total, level 2 on the vector --- and they are exactly
the two aggregates of rung one, in the order the paragraph above puts
them. Figure~\ref{fig:branching} draws them. The delivery rung is kept as a
third level of the tree, entered only where the first two have nothing
left to say: a region that fixes the whole fleet vector and whose
relaxation is still fractional.

\paragraph{Level 1: the total fleet.}
A bound on the total fleet has nothing to compensate it, as above.
Three regions partition the space:
\begin{itemize}
\item $X\ge X^{\star}+1$ costs a whole truck with no offset. A plan in
  it improves on the incumbent only if the extra truck saves more than
  $cK$ minus the incumbent's gap in inventory, and the region's
  relaxation says at once whether that is possible; it is usually
  pruned by bound.
\item $X=X^{\star}$ fixes transport. Everything inside is inventory
  allocation, and this is where the second level goes.
\item $X\le X^{\star}-1$ is the only region that can beat the incumbent
  outright --- ``is there a plan with fewer trucks?'' --- and is
  therefore worth a certificate of its own. It is also the region the
  tree constructs in \emph{first}: with the equality region on top of
  the stack its Murty subtree can take the whole budget while a smaller
  fleet is never asked for, which is what happened on a pure pool
  before the order was changed (Table~\ref{tab:pool}).
\end{itemize}
A plan found \emph{inside} an inequality region, with total $X_{2}$,
splits that region on its total exactly as the root was split ---
$[\,\cdot,X_{2}-1]$, $\{X_{2}\}$ and $[X_{2}+1,\cdot\,]$ cover it ---
and the equality piece starts a Murty chain of its own; level 1 is
recursive. A construction that fails inside a region says only that the
\emph{pool} cannot support that fleet, since step 2 is exact over the
pool; the region is therefore priced under its own duals with a small
share of the budget and constructed once more before it is given up,
and a bound proved by that pricing prunes it.

\paragraph{Level 2: the fleet vector, inside $X=X^{\star}$.}
Fix an order on the pairs $(g,t)$ --- periods first, then direction. The
fleet vector of any plan in the region differs from $\mathbf{n}^{\star}$
at a first index $j$, or nowhere. Branching at the first pair where a
solution may differ gives three children, $n_{j}\le n^{\star}_{j}-1$,
$n_{j}\ge n^{\star}_{j}+1$ and $n_{j}=n^{\star}_{j}$, and the equality
child is partitioned again at index $j+1$ (Murty's scheme). The regions
are disjoint and exhaustive, and the equality child is expanded first so
that the neighbourhood of the incumbent is searched before its
complement. The partition is taken around the plan \emph{constructed at
that node}, not around the global incumbent, so a node that improves the
incumbent is immediately re-partitioned around the better plan and
nothing has to be restarted. The chain is cut at a depth that rises by
two whenever the stack empties with budget left.

\paragraph{Processing a node.}
A node is a region imposed on the RMP as fleet rows. The LP over the
current pool is solved first; being an over-estimate of the region's LP
it is a filter and never a bound (Section \ref{sec:bounds}). If the
filter allows pruning and the certification policy admits the node,
column generation is run to a certificate; a certified value at or
above the incumbent prunes the node, an infeasible region is discarded,
and a certified value below the incumbent is recorded as that region's
bound. In every other case the node carries its parent's bound, which
remains valid for the child because the child's region is a subset of
the parent's. The flow-first relax and fix of Section
\ref{sec:relaxfix-flow} is then run inside the region, and if it returns
a plan cheaper than the incumbent the incumbent is replaced. Finally, if
the region fixes $X$, the Murty children are generated around the
constructed plan.

\paragraph{Two generators, one pool.}
Pricing returns extreme points of $\operatorname{conv}(\Omega^{g})$; the
relax and fix returns covers of a delivery vector, which are interior
points that no dual vector reaches (Figure~\ref{fig:hull}). A column is
a feasible load, and a node's constraints bound the multipliers
$\lambda$, not the load, so a column found anywhere is valid everywhere:
every column from either generator goes into the one shared pool, and a
node that is pruned still leaves its columns behind. That is the
mechanism by which the tree improves on the root. It is not that the
tree reaches an integral relaxation --- it essentially never does ---
but that each region it visits asks the construction for a plan under
constraints the root never saw, and the loads that plan needs are added
for every later node to use.

\paragraph{What the tree reports.}
The incumbent is the cheapest verified plan found by any construction.
The root bound is the certificate obtained before any branching ---
under 3D loading, that of the volumetric relaxation
(Proposition~\ref{prop:inclusion}). The global bound of a branch and
bound is the minimum over its \emph{frontier}, not its root: a region
nobody expanded contributes nothing, which is why a dive reports its
root value however far it has gone. At the end of the budget the regions
still open are therefore swept --- filtered by their pool LP and
certified where the filter allows --- and the global bound is the minimum
over the certified bounds of the open regions, floored at the root bound
and capped at the incumbent. Under 3D loading a region's certificate is
taken, like the root's, from the volumetric relaxation with the region's
fleet rows applied, which Proposition~\ref{prop:inclusion} covers: the
rows bound the fleet vector, not the load model, so the volumetric
master over the region bounds the 3D master over it. Optimality is
claimed \emph{only} when the root is certified, no region remains open,
no restart has broken the partition, and the incumbent meets the global
bound. No run reported in Section \ref{sec:results} meets these
conditions, so the method certifies no optimum anywhere in this paper;
the one optimum the paper does report is established by inclusion from
an exactly solved relaxation and not by the method
(Section~\ref{sec:res-vol}).

\subsection{The construction under 3D loading}
\label{sec:geo3d}

Step 3 of Section \ref{sec:relaxfix-flow} builds trucks from a delivery
vector with a volumetric notion of a legal load:
\eqref{eq:part-cap}--\eqref{eq:part-floor} are exactly the volumetric
column conditions. Over the volumetric oracle that notion \emph{is} the
definition of a column, and every part the partition proposes is a
column by construction. Under 3D loading a load must also satisfy
\eqref{eq:col-geom}--\eqref{eq:col-agg}. The volumetric conditions are
nevertheless \emph{necessary} for geometric feasibility
(Proposition~\ref{prop:inclusion}), so the partition programme is kept
as a \emph{relaxation} of the geometric partition and each part it
proposes is then tested for placeability. Step 2 makes no oracle call
and is unchanged, so the objective is still decided before any geometry
is examined; what changes is only whether the trucks that objective
implies can be built.

\paragraph{Deciding placeability.}
A part is submitted to a placeability oracle that returns one of three
answers. A constructive \emph{shelf design} --- a small integer
programme that lays the part's stacks in shelves along the trailer
length, one integer count per shelf, footprint and orientation ---
supplies essentially every positive answer, and its layout is passed
through the independent verifier of Section \ref{sec:results} before it
counts; a witness the verifier rejects is not a witness. The pricing
problem of Section \ref{sec:pp}, run in feasibility mode with the true
slot bound \eqref{sb:eq:Ibound}, supplies the \emph{infeasibility}
proofs; the true bound is what makes an infeasible verdict a proof,
since a model with fewer slots than a layout needs can report
infeasible while the load is placeable. A part left undecided within
the limit is neither accepted nor refuted. A placeable part becomes a
column with its layout as witness, exactly as a priced column does.

\paragraph{Two kinds of cut, and they are different claims.}
A part \emph{proved} unplaceable yields a \emph{dominance cut}
forbidding every load that dominates it componentwise. The cut is valid
because placeability is downward closed: removing an RTI from a
placeable layout leaves every position unchanged and every stack no
taller and no heavier, so a load dominating an unplaceable one is itself
unplaceable, and the cut removes the whole up-set of the refuted load
rather than a single point. The full-truckload floor is not part of the
placeability test, so it does not break the closure. A part left
\emph{undecided} is instead excluded as a single point, so that the
partition proposes something else. That is a restriction of a primal
heuristic and never a validity claim: it cannot make the method report
an invalid plan, only fail to find one. The two constraints are kept
separate because they rest on different grounds, and only the first may
ever be read as knowledge about the instance.

A dominance cut written at the refuted load itself is weaker than it
needs to be, and the difference matters on a multi-commodity part.
Before cutting, the refuted load is tightened \emph{along its ray}:
$\lfloor\sigma v\rfloor$ is probed for $\sigma$ bisected in $(0,1]$,
and the cut is written at the smallest scaled-down point still
\emph{proved} unplaceable. Every point of the one-unit-at-a-time
staircase the partition would otherwise walk down --- $\{R_{1}:272\}$,
$271$, $270,\dots$ on one measured pair --- dominates that point, so one
cut removes the staircase instead of its top step. The bisection is
seven probes at most, its placeable verdicts are cached like any other,
and an \emph{undecided} probe stops it at the last proof, so the cut is
never written anywhere that was not proved. Without this step a
reimplementation reproduces the staircase and exhausts its round cap
without reaching the answer.

\paragraph{Placeability caps.}
The maximum count of a single commodity in one truck is a scalar, and by
the same closure it is monotone, so it is bisected once per direction
and commodity at the start of a run --- a proved pricing optimum tightens
it, an undecided probe leaves the analytic cap in force --- and handed to
the partition as the bound $\bar u_{gc}$ before it solves. Without it a
single-commodity dominance cut advances one unit per round, from the
volumetric fill down towards the placeable count, at a placeability call
and a partition re-solve each. A cap is also a measurement in its own
right: it is the largest number of one commodity that \emph{can} be
placed, and setting it beside the number a volumetric capacity credits
measures the relaxation's optimism directly, with no plan involved.
Section~\ref{sec:res-abstraction} reports that comparison.

\paragraph{A stack relaxation inside the partition.}
The partition additionally carries, per truck and footprint, an integer
number of stacks, with the crush limit and the height budget imposed per
stack and the stacks' floor areas summed against the trailer floor;
summed over the $n_{gt}$ trucks of a period the same rows sit inside the
aggregate master of step 2, with one integer count per footprint and
period. The rows are necessary --- every truck of the period has its own
integer stack counts satisfying the per-truck rows, and summing them over
the period's trucks gives exactly the aggregate rows --- and they are
precisely what the volumetric relaxation omits: a family whose RTIs are
short enough for four layers by height but heavy enough for only two by
the crush limit is credited by volume with twice its placeable count.
The layer coefficient must be the \emph{binding} one, the smaller of the
height-limited and crush-limited counts; written with the height count
alone the row is not a necessary condition and cuts off feasible fleets.
The relaxation says nothing about the two-dimensional arrangement of the
stacks, which the placeability test and the dominance cuts cover.

\paragraph{The pool and the bound.}
The starting pool is the placeable single-commodity trucks of Section
\ref{sec:initcols} and nothing else; every other column comes from the
pricing ladder or from the relax and fix. On a catalogue where the crush
limit binds, the restricted master over that pool is not even feasible
--- no combination of pure trucks covers demand within the fleet --- so
clearing the artificials is the first thing column generation has to do.
\emph{Either} generator does it: at the per-call budget of
Section~\ref{sec:results} the exact oracle alone clears them in one
sweep, and so does one rung-fed sweep. What the rung buys is not
feasibility but a tighter restricted master per unit of work, and
Section~\ref{sec:res-3d} measures that
(Table~\ref{tab:ladder}). The root bound is the certified value of the
volumetric relaxation (Proposition~\ref{prop:inclusion}); geometric
pricing, given a share of the root budget, supplies columns and nothing
else. Section \ref{sec:results} reports what it supplies.

\subsection{Two corrections to the compact model}
\label{sec:corrections}

Implementing 3D-SCPTOP surfaced two points worth recording, both
invisible at $lt=1$.

\paragraph{Inventory recursion under a lead time.}
Constraints \eqref{sb:eq:inv-plant-main} and
\eqref{sb:eq:inve-sup-main} carry inventory forward from period $t-1$
and delay only the \emph{arrival}, which is what the physical system
does. An earlier statement of the model carried the stock itself
forward from $t-lt$,
\begin{align}
I_{pnt}  &=I_{p,n,t-lt}-d_{pnt}+\overline{V}_{n,t-lt}
    &&\forall n\in N,\; t\ge lt+1,
    \label{eq:fix-plant}\\
Ie_{str} &=Ie_{s,t-lt,r}+\overline{V^{\mathrm{e}}}_{r,t-lt}
          -\textstyle\sum_{n\in M(r)} d_{snt}
    &&\forall r\in R,\; t\ge lt+1,
    \label{eq:fix-sup}
\end{align}
which silently discards $lt-1$ periods of stock. With $lt=1$ the two
versions coincide, which is why the defect survived; every model in
this paper is run at $lt=1$, so no reported number changes either way.
The correction is recorded because the formulation is the contribution.

\paragraph{Coverage stock on folded RTIs.}
Constraint \eqref{sb:eq:safety-empty} requires $Ie_{gtr}\ge sse_{gtr}$ at
\emph{both} nodes, and every model in this paper enforces it that way:
the master of Section \ref{sec:mp}, the volumetric benchmark of
Appendix \ref{appendix:A}, the company procedure PLC-SCPTOP and the
compact model itself. An earlier implementation imposed the folded bound
at the supplier only, on the argument that the plant has no operational
use for a stock of empties and that the extra row could render an
instance with a tight RTI fleet infeasible. The row is what the model
states, so it was reinstated, and the concern did not materialise: on
the largest instances of Section \ref{sec:results} the root LP value is
identical with and without it, no coverage row carries artificial slack,
and the row is tight in a minority of periods without moving the
optimum. Enforcing it everywhere also removes an asymmetry: had the
master enforced the plant side and the comparison models not, the
comparison would have been unfair in the master's favour.
